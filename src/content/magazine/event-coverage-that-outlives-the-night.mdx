The most expensive mistake in event content is treating the event as the
deadline. The night is not the deliverable — it is the shoot.

## Before: decide the shot list like a heist

Great event coverage is planned like a heist, not improvised like a party.
Before anyone arrives we know the five moments that must exist: the room
empty and lit, the first arrival, the host mid-laugh, the product in hands,
the goodbye at the door. Everything else is bonus.

## During: publish while it is still true

Stories posted during the event do something no recap can — they make the
people who did not come watch it live. A two-person team splits the job: one
captures for tonight, one captures for the month ahead. The tonight feed is
fast and imperfect on purpose; urgency reads as honesty.

## After: the month of releases

- **Day one:** the recap reel, cut before sunrise while the night still trends.
- **Week one:** guest moments and details, tagged and shareable.
- **Weeks two to four:** the slower stories — the making-of, the portraits,
  the pieces that belong to the brand rather than the party.

> Four hours of event, thirty days of presence. That ratio is the whole job.

An event that only produces a same-night photo dump spent its budget on one
evening. Structured capture turns the same budget into a month of content —
and the next event starts with an audience that remembers the last one.
