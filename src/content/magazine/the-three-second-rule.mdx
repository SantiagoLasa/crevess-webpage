Nobody decides to watch a reel. They decide not to leave it — and they make
that decision in about three seconds. Everything we produce is built around
that window.

## The opening frame is a promise

The first frame has one job: tell the viewer what staying will get them. Not
the brand name, not a logo animation — a promise. Movement toward something,
a question the eye wants answered, a texture worth touching. If the first
frame could be a poster for the next twenty seconds, it is doing its job.

## Three hooks that consistently hold

- **The unfinished action.** A pour halfway poured, a door half open, a look
  half turned. The brain stays to see it complete.
- **The reframe.** Open on the detail, not the scene — the hands, not the
  chef; the stitch, not the dress. Context arriving late is context earned.
- **The cut on rhythm.** Two fast cuts in the first two seconds teach the
  viewer the video has a pulse. Silence after movement works the same way.

## What the numbers actually reward

> Watch time is not a metric. It is the metric wearing different costumes.

Completion rate, shares, follows — every downstream number improves when the
first three seconds are engineered instead of hoped for. In practice we
storyboard hooks before anything else: the last thing we decide about a reel
is everything after second three.
